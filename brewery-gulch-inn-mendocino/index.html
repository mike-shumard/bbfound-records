Change: +56.7 percentage points (absolute difference, not a multiplier).

A controlled experiment on the Perplexity API (Sonar), web search enabled, low search context, September 24, 2026: 15 properties × 4 questions × 2 repetitions = 120 scored property observations per condition. The intervention was records supplied directly to the AI in the prompt. It is not measured improvement from publishing records online — supplying records directly to AI does not prove that publishing a page on the web will cause AI to retrieve it. The ordinary-search control and the supplied-record condition each used 40 distinct AI calls; the peer-record condition used 120 calls. From the ordinary-search control to the supplied-record condition, 14 of the 15 properties improved and one tied. This is not a promise of any change for your property.

Secondary comparison — competitor records as the baseline. The same study ran a tougher baseline where the model received records for the 4 nearby properties but not the target’s record (PEERS-ONLY): 3 of 120 scored property observations named the target — 2.5%. Against the same supplied-record condition (FULL-LAYER: 95 of 120 scored property observations, 79.2%), the change was +76.7 percentage points, and all 15 properties moved in the same direction; the weakest effect was The Madrona (+2 of 8). “All 15 improved” describes this competitor-record comparison only — it does not apply to the ordinary-search comparison above.

An earlier, smaller study (5 properties; 50 observations per condition; no records supplied vs. all 5 market records supplied) found naming rise from 28 of 50 (56%) to 46 of 50 (92%), and top-recommendation rate from 5 of 50 (10%) to 23 of 50 (46%). Caveat from the original report: the lift was not target-specific — peer properties’ naming rose too (40 of 200 peer slots, 20%, to 106 of 200, 53%) — and the top-pick lift is confounded by design (the questions were written so the target was a plausible answer).
